Smart Glasses

An exploration of interface behavior when the screen is no longer the user's primary environment—and when attention itself becomes the most valuable design constraint.

Wearable interactionSupport the world; do not replace it.

Lightweight cues with deeper information available by intent.

Earn attention in the context of real life.

Smart glasses change a fundamental assumption in interface design: the user is not looking at a screen in isolation. They are walking, talking, driving, working or interacting with other people while information competes for attention.

The challenge is therefore not how much information can be displayed. It is how little information can create meaningful value without becoming distracting, intrusive or socially uncomfortable.

My role: define the behavior of the interface

I framed the concept around attention, context and intent. Instead of designing a miniature dashboard, I explored how the system could surface a useful cue, allow the wearer to decide whether to go deeper, and disappear when it no longer deserves attention.

Strategy: start light and make depth intentional

The experience uses contextual cues and layered information. The smallest useful signal appears first; richer information is available only when requested. Dismissible states and clear transitions keep the wearer in control.

Signal

Surface only information that is relevant enough to interrupt the user's attention.

Explore

Let intent determine whether the user sees more detail.

Exit

Make dismissal and return to the physical environment effortless.

Human factors define the product

A viable wearable experience needs strong privacy controls, accessibility, safety considerations, social awareness and explicit attention management. These are product requirements, not edge cases. They determine whether the interface can earn trust in everyday life.

Leadership impact

This work reflects a systems approach to emerging technology: design the interaction, but also the human, social and environmental constraints that make the interaction viable. Leadership means defining those boundaries before the team gets attached to the technology.
