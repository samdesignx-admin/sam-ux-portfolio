Wearable UX · Emerging interfaces

Smart Glasses

An exploration of interface behavior when the screen is no longer the user’s primary environment.

Spatial UIAttention designHuman-centered AI
ProblemStrategyHuman factorsOutcome
Wearable interactionSupport the world; do not replace it.

Lightweight cues with deeper information available by intent.

Earn attention in the context of real life.

Smart glasses compete with the physical world for attention. Unlike a phone, they cannot assume a person has entered a dedicated screen, so relevance and restraint become core interaction requirements.

Strategy: start light and make depth intentional

The concept uses contextual cues and layered information: the smallest useful signal appears first, while richer detail is available only when requested. Dismissible states keep the wearer in control of attention.

Human factors define the product

A real product would need strong privacy controls, accessibility, safety considerations and explicit attention management. These are not edge cases; they determine whether a wearable interface is trustworthy in everyday use.

Leadership lens

This work reflects a systems approach to emerging technology: design the interaction, but also the social and human constraints that make it viable.
