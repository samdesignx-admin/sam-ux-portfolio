One recognizable entry point for a richer, measurable ad experience.

I started with a simple observation: advertising disappears too quickly.

A person can notice an ad on a billboard, in print, on TV or on social—and then the moment is gone. The idea for ADKey came from asking a practical question: what if every one of those moments had a clear, consistent way to continue?

Where I began

I did not begin with a dashboard or a QR code. I began with the handoff between an ad impression and the person who is interested enough to act. The goal was to make that handoff understandable in any channel, without asking audiences to learn a different behavior each time.

How I approached it

I reduced the interaction to one reusable product primitive: an AdKey. An advertiser creates an experience, assigns it a key and distributes that same key wherever the campaign appears. For the audience, the pattern is equally direct: scan or enter the key, then continue the experience in the right context.

For the advertiser

Create the destination, assign the identity and manage the campaign from one workflow.

For the audience

Use a familiar action to move from a physical or digital impression into a richer experience.

For the product

Connect scans, views, clicks and downstream actions to the same campaign identity.

What I’m building

ADKey is an ecosystem, not a single screen. It needs an advertiser workflow, a public entry experience and a shared model for campaign identity. The visual direction—bold black and yellow—supports that role: it should feel recognizable, direct and built to travel across channels.

Why it matters in my portfolio

This is the kind of problem I enjoy leading: find the overlooked moment in a system, define a simple interaction model, and align the experience around it—from product strategy and information architecture to the details people actually use.
