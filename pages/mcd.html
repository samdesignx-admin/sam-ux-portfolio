Conversation designShort turns. Clear confirmation.

Voice interaction designed around ambiguity, correction and pace.

Design a conversation that works while attention is divided.

Drive-thru ordering happens in motion, with environmental noise and little patience for long flows. The experience must recognize intent, support changes and communicate a reliable final order without demanding a person’s full attention.

Strategy: structure the conversation around confidence

Short turns identify intent, confirm critical choices, handle corrections and summarize before submission. Confirmation is a product pattern, not an afterthought: it gives people clear moments to correct quantities, modifiers and the final order.

The interface is only one part of the service

The concept connects the conversational layer to the operational flow of the drive-thru. That service-design view prevents an elegant voice exchange from creating confusion downstream for customers or staff.

Leadership lens

A focused demonstration of how I adapt UX strategy to an unfamiliar interface: start with context, define recovery, and align the customer interaction with the service that must deliver it.
