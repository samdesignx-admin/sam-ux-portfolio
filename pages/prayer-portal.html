Community UX · Service design

Prayer Portal

A digital community experience built around requests, participation and the care required when people share something personal.

Information architectureCommunity UXTrust design
ProblemStrategyExperienceOutcome
Community participationAsk. Respond. Return.

A calm structure for private intent and visible acts of support.

Design participation without turning care into a feed.

A prayer community has a different emotional contract from a typical social product. People need a clear way to make a request, understand what happens next, and participate without exposing more than they intend. The design problem is as much about tone and trust as it is about navigation.

Strategy: make purpose and privacy legible

The experience is organized around a small set of meaningful actions: create or discover a request, offer support, and return to the community. Clear hierarchy and intentionally modest interaction patterns keep the focus on the person and the request—not activity for its own sake.

Service-design lens

The interface is only one part of the experience. Content states, participation cues and notification moments need to work together so members understand the community’s norms and can act with confidence. That systems view creates room for connection while respecting emotional context.

Leadership lens

This concept demonstrates how I translate an ambiguous human need into a structured service: defining the primary journeys, setting the trust boundaries, and designing a cohesive experience rather than a collection of screens.
