Home-buying UX · Product strategy

KeyPath

A guided home-buying experience built around a simple leadership principle: when a journey is complex, the product should help people understand where they are, what matters now and what comes next.

Home-buying journeyGive the next decision a clear path.

A product system organized around orientation, choices and momentum.

Home buying is not one task. It is a chain of decisions.

People move through discovery, evaluation, financing and commitment while managing unfamiliar terminology, competing information and significant personal stakes. A conventional page-by-page experience can make each interaction understandable while still leaving the overall journey confusing.

KeyPath reframes the product as a guided journey: establish orientation first, expose the decision that matters now, and give users enough context to move forward without removing their control.

My role: turn journey complexity into product structure

I approached the work at the journey and information-architecture level before treating it as a visual design exercise. The key leadership decision was to define the experience around user progress and decision confidence rather than around the organization's internal categories.

Strategy: orient first, then reveal the right next step

The experience uses progressive disclosure to balance guidance and autonomy. Users first understand their position in the journey, then see the information and action relevant to that stage. More detailed content is available when invited rather than competing with the primary decision.

Orient

Show where the user is and what the current stage means.

Decide

Make the immediate choice understandable and expose the information needed to make it.

Advance

Make the next step visible so momentum comes from clarity rather than pressure.

From journey model to interaction system

The project translates the journey into a dedicated web experience with reusable structure, responsive behavior and a consistent visual language. The system is designed to support the content hierarchy rather than decorate it: status, choice, supporting information and action each have a clear role.

Leadership impact

KeyPath demonstrates how I work through product ambiguity: define the journey, identify the decisions that create friction, align the information model to those decisions, and give the team a repeatable structure for designing the experience.
